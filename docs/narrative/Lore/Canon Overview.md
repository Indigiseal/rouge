# Canon Overview

## Short version

One day, beside the ordinary moon, a **second** one appeared — a smooth glowing sphere circling it in a perfect orbit. From that night on, the world stopped living by the old calendar: a new month lasts **fifteen days**, the time of one full turn of the sphere.

When the Second Moon first appeared, a stone fell from the sky. People later named it the [[The Waystar|Waystar]]. It does not speak, sing, or carve omens into walls. It simply **points the way** — always in one direction, toward a place no one knows for certain, remembered in legend as [[The Far Place]].

At the start of every new month, the world fills with a new kind of danger. In one turn it is beasts and thorns; in another, silk and depth; in a third, tolls, raids, and the open road. Each month-name is not a date so much as a **face of threat** the world must survive for the next fifteen days.

The hero follows the Waystar because it is the only thing in the world that leads **not away from danger, but through it**. The goal is to reach the Far Place, learn the origin of the Second Moon, and find a way to end its influence before catastrophe becomes the calendar itself.

If the hero dies, the Waystar returns them to life at the beginning of the next month. Every run is therefore not a new story, but **another attempt to walk the same road under a different sky**.

---

## Core fantasy pillars

- The world is broken not by war or plague, but by a **new law of the heavens**.
- The calendar itself has become part of the threat.
- Every floor is **one lived day** on the road.
- Every act is **one month** of the new world.
- Death does not cancel the journey; it only shifts the hero into the next month.
