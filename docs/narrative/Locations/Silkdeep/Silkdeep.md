# Silkdeep

> [!info] Location — Act 1 (wrong road)
> A human palace whose final feast still sits at table beneath silk. Not the Waystar's road. See [[The True Path]], [[Locations Index]], [[Location Rule]].

Silkdeep is a **human baron's palace**, abandoned for years but caught in the
exact posture of its final celebration. Tables remain laid. Musicians hang above
their instruments. Guests sit inside upright cocoons. Spider eggs fill serving
dishes and wine cabinets. The deeper rooms still resemble a feast, but one now
maintained for the colony rather than the court.

The Waystar door opens as the inner door of a crypt on the palace's rear
grounds. To a first-time player this is simply another strange world behind a door. Its deeper importance — that this
was [[The Magus|the future Magus's]] home before the Second Moon — must remain
hidden until a later reveal.

## Theme

**Certainty becomes captivity.** The baron surrendered responsibility to
predictions; the guests became permanent figures in a feast they expected to
control. Silk preserves the instant when ownership reversed.

## Place

### The Palace of the Last Feast

Before the fall, the palace was famous for its astrologer, its exotic menagerie,
and feasts extravagant enough to advertise the baron's prosperity. Its public
rooms are human and opulent: galleries, kitchens, guest wings, a ballroom,
banquet halls, a counting room, and the magician's tower. The colony has turned
those rooms into a vertical web of silk corridors, egg nurseries, and suspended
prey. “Deep” means progression farther into this preserved social world and
into the web of its past, not a descent through ordinary caves.

Roster encountered here: [[Spider]], [[Cave Crawler]], [[Silk Husk]], [[Stinger Scorpion]], [[Silkslinger]].
Boss: [[Spider Queen]].

## Arrival scene — locked sequence

This is the mandatory scene immediately after the player chooses Silkdeep. It
establishes the palace and unease, but explains none of its history.

### Visual sequence

1. The player passes through a heavy stone Waystar door wound in old webbing.
2. On the other side, the player appears to be **walking out of a crypt**, as
   though the portal had always been the crypt's inner door.
3. The small crypt stands in the neglected rear grounds of a vast human palace.
4. Walking around it reveals the palace properly: roofs, walls, windows, and
   statues are covered in pale webbing. No spiders are visible.
5. The player crosses the silent grounds to the main entrance. Its massive,
   weighty doors stand slightly ajar.
6. The gap is narrow but passable. The player squeezes through rather than
   opening the doors.
7. Beyond lies the great entrance hall, abandoned and thick with webbing.
   A pair of monumental semicircular staircases rises toward the second floor.
8. This is the first complete view of the location: a deserted palace rather
   than a cave or nest.
9. Small rustles begin in the corners. Nothing attacks yet. Points of reflected
   light — too regularly paired to be dust or glass — watch from the darkness.

### Narrative constraints

- Do not show a full spider before gameplay begins. The absence of spiders in
  such an enormous web is the scene's central tension.
- Do not show cocooned feast guests yet; the scene should leave that discovery
  for the route inside the palace.
- Do not name the baron, court magician, Crown Weaver, Magus, or Second Moon.
- The crypt is an arrival threshold, not proof that the hero was buried there.
  The Waystar door folds space so the crossing reads locally as an exit from it.
- End on the watching eyes. The first combat encounter pays off the threat.

## Before the feast

The baron's court magician kept many rare spiders and read possible futures in
their webs. He advised the baron on:

- when to sell or retain the harvest;
- which caravan routes would return safely;
- which houses would honour debts and treaties;
- which marriages and appointments would strengthen the court;
- which rivals would move against him.

The webs showed **probabilities**, not one infallible future. Their advice was
usually good, and that success made the baron reckless. He borrowed against
predicted harvests, spent the profit of caravans before they returned, and
removed every reserve that appeared unnecessary. Several correct predictions
were therefore enough to build one fragile structure in which a single delay
could make every debt fall due at once.

When the magician's answers became less useful and his attention turned to a
private work, the baron's finances collapsed. The baron decided that one spider
from the magician's collection — the priceless **Crown Weaver** — could be sold
to a foreign collector for enough money to cover his debts.

He arranged a great feast as cover. The magician would be occupied at the high
table, servants and strangers could move unnoticed, and the buyer could leave
with the spider before dawn.

## The last feast

The theft removed the centre of the collection's web. Small spiders hidden
throughout the palace began to grow; eggs opened in walls, furniture, vents,
decorative silk, and the magician's rooms. The colony descended on a palace
full of warm bodies.

The disaster came in stages: threads joined cups across the tables; bows stuck
to musicians' hands; doors sealed from the outside; tiny spiders fell from the
ceiling; then the great spiders came down. The foreign buyer never escaped.
The Crown Weaver fed on the panic and accumulated divination magic and became
the [[Spider Queen]].

The colony did not destroy the celebration. It **kept it**:

- cocooned guests remain seated at their assigned places;
- dancers are wrapped together in pairs;
- musicians hang over silent instruments;
- goblets, chandeliers, and silver covers anchor the web;
- egg clutches occupy serving bowls and warming cabinets;
- some human shapes still move after several years;
- spiders arrange bodies and tableware as if continuing the service.

The visual rule is: **the human feast never ended; it changed hosts.**

## Hidden authorial truth — later reveal

This section is not knowledge available to the hero during the first Silkdeep
run.

The court magician had already heard the unknown whisper promising the **Age of
Confluence**: the rise of the Second Moon, the merging of all worlds, and the
arrival of a ruler capable of great works. He chose to serve that promised
power and eventually became the [[The Magus|Magus]], its deputy and right hand.

The baron's collapse was not simply bad luck. The magician used individually
truthful short-term predictions to make the baron dependent, overextended, and
desperate. He knew desperation would turn the baron toward the Crown Weaver.
The theft, the crowded feast, and the awakening colony formed an early step in
the work that would eventually raise the Second Moon. Whether the feast was a
required sacrifice, an experiment in merging possible worlds, or both remains
open until the Moon ritual is designed.

The magician's body is therefore never found in Silkdeep. Early clues must
support an innocent reading as well as the later truth:

- his private rooms were emptied selectively, not ransacked;
- the spider cages appear opened from within;
- the baron's debt ledgers contain several recommendations in the magician's hand;
- the final web in the observatory resembles the Second Moon only in hindsight;
- surviving messages refer to a promised “joining” without explaining it.

No Act 1 scene may call him the Magus, show his later face unambiguously, or
explain the Confluence. Silkdeep should first read as a tragedy caused by a
greedy baron and dangerous divination. Later knowledge turns it into a planned
crime and a flashback to the antagonist's origin.

## Features

- Preserved human luxury continually collides with colony behaviour.
- Silk is curtain, tablecloth, corridor, restraint, archive, and nest.
- Divination imagery appears before its meaning is understood.
- The magician is present through absences and traces, never a direct reveal.
- Movement goes from public celebration toward private accounts, menagerie,
  observatory, and finally the Queen's transformed banquet hall.

- [[The Silk Cache]] — A chamber of cocoons and abandoned arms. Leave, crack them open in a fight, or burn the nest with Fireball.
