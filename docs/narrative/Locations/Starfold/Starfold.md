# Starfold

> [!info] Location — Act 3 (**true road**)
> The Magus's house. The deity the goblins named and the fair sold. See [[The True Path]], [[Locations Index]].

You step **through** the wound in the sky. The Second Moon is a building: galleries along the orbit, windows onto the ordinary moon from the wrong side, stairs on a perfect curve. This is the country inside the sphere — not a crater, not a reflection.

People who have only seen the sphere from below call this a myth. The Path does not. One of the three last roads opens here.

## Theme

The sky's law, made local. Gravity is a suggestion. Rooms remember constellations. Distance is a spell someone already cast.

## Place

### The Inner Sky

**The Inner Sky** is the signature of this location.

It does not look like night with extra stars. It looks like a **working**: galleries of orbit, halls whose windows open onto the ordinary moon from the wrong side, stairs that climb around a perfect curve. Something here was built, or grown, or argued into being.

This is the demesne of [[The Magus]]. They are not written yet. Until they are, treat the Magus as: *the one who keeps the sphere's law, and who lives where that law is thickest.*

Starfold is one door to [[The Far Place]]. Spherefall is the same wound from outside. Mirrorwane is the wound wearing the hero's face. All three can end an expedition. Only this one is the Magus's house.

## Intensity

Act 3. The Near Road is gone. The hunting land is gone. Here the world no longer agrees that a road should be dirt.

- Things stand that have no weight.
- Light arrives before its source.
- Familiar tools work, then work *too well*, then refuse.
- The Waystar is loud here. It fell from this kind of sky.

## Features

- Architecture of orbit: curves, folds, rooms that are also positions in the sky.
- The Second Moon is not "above." It is the building.
- The Magus's working is visible as weather (later: specific magic, servants, bargains).
- Title card should feel like stepping off the world, not into a deeper dungeon.
